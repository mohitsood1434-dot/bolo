The other kind of worry

"I don't know what I want to do" is a normal sentence.

You are being asked to pick a life at fifteen. Nobody actually does that in one go — they take a step, learn something, and adjust.

Ask better questions

Not "what should I become" — try "which class do I stop checking the clock in?"

Test, don't decide

One week of actually trying a thing tells you more than a month of thinking about it.

Separate two fears

"I'll choose wrong" and "they'll be disappointed" are different problems. They need different conversations.

Nothing is final

Streams, degrees and first jobs all get changed by people every single year. A step is not a sentence.

The hard part

Borrow a first sentence.

Most people don't stay silent because they have nothing to say. They stay silent because the opening line is missing. Take one of these.

To a parent

"I want to tell you something, and I need you to just listen first — I'm not looking for a solution yet."

To a teacher

"Ma'am, can I talk to you for two minutes after class? Something has been affecting my studies."

To a friend

"This is going to sound random, but I've been feeling low for a while and I didn't know who to tell."

To yourself, at 2am

"This feeling is real, and it is also temporary. I don't have to solve it tonight."

Talk to someone

This app is a first step, not the whole road.
